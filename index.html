When Imperfect Automated Fact-Checking Backfires

Social learning in the presence of misinformation

Zixuan Liu Ming Cao

Faculty of Science and Engineering, University of Groningen, The Netherlands

arXiv PDF Page source
  • +More people check. A free automated fact-checker lets agents verify an article who otherwise would not, and some of them escape a wrong cascade.
  • −But its mistakes spread. A private verdict is invisible to others; only the like or dislike it triggers is seen. An error can start a wrong cascade that perfect manual checking would have avoided.
  • =Net effect. For articles of intermediate reliability the tool strictly lowers the probability of a correct cascade, and on average it helps only once it is accurate enough.

Interactive 1

Why would anyone check, and why do cascades stop checking?

An agent checks when the expected gain beats the cost. Those who are already nearly sure gain nothing, so the most ambivalent agents check. As the crowd becomes convinced, the group of checkers shrinks to nothing: that is what a cascade is.
