When Imperfect Automated Fact-Checking Backfires

Social learning in the presence of misinformation

Zixuan Liu Ming Cao

Faculty of Science and Engineering, University of Groningen, The Netherlands

arXiv PDF Page source
  • +More people check. A free automated fact-checker lets agents verify an article who otherwise would not, and some of them escape a wrong cascade.
  • −But its mistakes spread. A wrong private verdict can start a wrong cascade that perfect manual checking would have avoided.
  • =Net effect. Under appropriate conditions, adding the tool strictly lowers the probability of a correct cascade for articles of intermediate reliability; and on average it helps only once it is accurate enough.

Interactive 1

Everybody herds, and cascade arises

Agents arrive one at a time and see only what the earlier agents did. Each can pay a cost to check the article with a fact-checker that has two-sided error rates. The decision rule is induced by the expected payoff, and describes what an agent does according to her posterior belief that the article is fake; the simulation is a crowd following that rule. The line tracks the public belief that the article is truthful; once it enters a shaded zone, nobody checks any more and the cascade arises.

What to look for. Only agents who are unsure check: someone who is nearly sure gains nothing from a check. Early on many agents check and the public belief moves fast; once it is so strong that no one’s own belief falls in the checking band any more, nobody checks and the crowd herds. Try “Free but error-prone”, where everyone in the middle checks but some checks are wrong, and the article presets, such as a reliable article that is fake. The numbers at the bottom say how often crowds end in a right or a wrong cascade.
